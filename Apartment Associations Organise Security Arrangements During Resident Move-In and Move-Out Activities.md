# How Can Apartment Associations Organise Security Arrangements During Resident Move-In and Move-Out Activities?

Resident move-in and move-out activities bring unusual levels of movement into an apartment complex. Moving trucks, packers, furniture, appliances, contractors and visitors may arrive within a short period, sometimes using the same entrance as residents and daily deliveries. Without a clear process, vehicle queues can build up, common areas may become obstructed and security staff may struggle to confirm which movements have been approved.

Apartment associations can manage these periods more smoothly by introducing a simple booking and approval process, defining vehicle routes and time slots, documenting goods movement and assigning clear responsibilities to security personnel, facility staff and residents. A planned approach to apartment move-in move-out security helps maintain orderly access while respecting residents' convenience and privacy.

## 1. Establish a Prior Booking and Approval Process

The association or property manager should ask residents to notify the management office before a planned move. The request can include the apartment number, resident's name, proposed date and time, expected duration, moving company details and approximate vehicle size. Where a tenant is moving in or out, management can confirm that the request has been authorised through the property's normal resident or owner verification process.

Set a reasonable notice period and explain how urgent or last-minute requests will be handled. Share the approved time slot with the gate team and facility staff so that they can prepare for the arrival. A central register or digital request form can help avoid situations where a resident has approval but the entrance team has not been informed.

The process should be consistent for owners and tenants. Security personnel should verify the approval using the association's agreed procedure rather than making informal decisions based on familiarity with a person or vehicle.

## 2. Allocate Dedicated Time Slots for Moving Vehicles

Moving vehicles can occupy entrance lanes, loading areas and internal driveways for longer than ordinary visitor vehicles. Associations should define suitable time windows for major moves, taking into account school transport, office-hour traffic, waste collection, emergency access and other routine activities.

Where possible, schedule only the number of major moves that the building can safely accommodate at one time. If two or more towers share a narrow driveway or service entrance, coordinate their schedules to prevent congestion. Communicate the approved arrival window to the resident and moving company in advance.

The association should also identify a waiting location for vehicles that arrive early. Drivers should not block the main gate, emergency access routes, ramps or pedestrian paths while waiting for permission to enter.

## 3. Define Vehicle Routes and Loading or Unloading Areas

Before moving activity begins, the facility team should identify the permitted route from the gate to the designated loading or unloading point. Consider basement height restrictions, turning space, driveway width, lift access and areas where large vehicles could interfere with normal traffic.

If the property has a service lift, reserve it according to the association's procedure and communicate any protective arrangements for lift interiors, corridors and wall corners. Where no service lift is available, specify the approved lift or staircase arrangements and any limits that apply.

Use clear instructions for drivers and movers. Security guards can direct vehicles to the approved location, while facility staff can help coordinate lift availability and protect common areas. The route should always preserve access for emergency vehicles and residents who need to enter or leave the building.

## 4. Assign Clear Responsibilities to Security and Facility Teams

Apartment security guards should not be expected to manage every part of a resident's move. Their role in the process is to control and record access according to the association's instructions, direct vehicles and report deviations. Facility personnel can coordinate service lifts, loading areas, floor protection and maintenance-related concerns.

A practical responsibility split may include:

- **Association or property manager:** Approve the request, communicate rules and resolve exceptions.
- **Gate security team:** Verify approval, record vehicle details, notify the relevant team and guide the driver.
- **Facility or maintenance team:** Prepare common areas, coordinate lift use and check for damage after the move.
- **Resident:** Ensure movers follow the approved time, route and building rules.
- **Moving company:** Follow site instructions, supervise its workers and keep materials within the permitted area.

Share a contact list with the people on duty. If a vehicle arrives without approval, the gate team should follow a defined escalation route rather than allowing entry or refusing access without checking with the authorised manager.

## 5. Maintain a Goods Movement and Vehicle Entry Record

A clear record helps the association understand who entered the property, which apartment the move relates to and when the vehicle left. The register may capture the date, time, apartment number, resident or authorised contact, vehicle registration, moving company, entry approval and exit time.

For goods movement monitoring, record details that are proportionate to the property's needs and consistent with its approved policy. The purpose is to maintain an accountable movement record, not to create unnecessary inventories of residents' personal belongings. If photographs or supporting documents are required under association rules, explain the reason and handle them with appropriate care.

Security personnel should record exceptions such as an unapproved additional vehicle, a change in the moving company or use of a different exit route. The supervisor or property manager can review unusual entries and resolve questions with the resident.

## 6. Protect Common Areas and Set Rules for Contractors

Moving teams may use trolleys, tools and packing materials that can affect walls, flooring, lifts and fire doors. Associations should communicate practical rules before the move, including where materials may be temporarily placed, how waste must be removed and which areas must remain clear.

The facility team can inspect the planned route before and after a move, noting any existing damage and any new issue that needs attention. If protective sheets or lift padding are required, clarify who will arrange them and who will remove them after the work.

Contractors should be instructed not to prop open controlled doors, enter unrelated floors or leave equipment unattended in common areas. These expectations support residential premises security while helping keep shared spaces usable for all residents.

## 7. Prepare for Delays, Disputes and Emergency Access

Even a well-planned move can run late or encounter unexpected problems. The association should decide how to handle a delayed vehicle, a move that exceeds its approved slot, lift breakdowns or disagreement between residents and movers. Provide a contact person who can approve a revised schedule or arrange an alternative.

Security staff should know how to report blocked corridors, damaged property, unsafe moving practices or vehicles obstructing access. Emergency routes and exits must remain available throughout the activity. If an urgent safety issue arises, staff should follow the property's emergency procedures and contact the appropriate responsible personnel.

After the move, the facility team can close the request by confirming that the vehicle has exited, shared areas are clear and any reported damage has been recorded for follow-up.

## A More Organised Approach to Residential Move Management

A consistent move-in and move-out procedure helps apartment associations balance access control with the practical needs of residents. Advance booking, defined vehicle slots, approved routes, clear team responsibilities and accurate entry records reduce uncertainty during busy periods. Regular communication also helps residents and moving companies understand what is expected before arriving at the gate.
